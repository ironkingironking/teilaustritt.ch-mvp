      Church tax: the essentials

      Only members of a recognised church pay church tax. This follows from freedom of religion and conscience (Art. 15 of the Federal Constitution). Each canton regulates the details itself, so there are 26 different systems.

      When do you stop paying church tax?

      According to the Federal Supreme Court, tax liability ends as soon as your declaration has reached the responsible office. The cantons apply this differently:

      Immediately on receipt

        For the whole year of leaving

        If you leave during the year, liability ends retroactively on 31 December of the previous year.

          Different system

          No church tax, voluntary payment or only in some municipalities.

            How is church tax calculated?

            Usually as a percentage of the simple cantonal tax; in some cantons as a multiple of it or as a share of the cantonal tax owed. It is based on income and wealth, in some cantons also on real estate gains.

            The rate is usually set every year by the parish assembly, so it differs from parish to parish.

            I leave, my spouse or partner stays a member

            Married couples and registered partners are taxed jointly, but the church tax is split. After you leave, only the share of the person who stays a member is taxed, usually at half the rate of their denomination.

            Some cantons also take the children and their denomination into account (split per head or in thirds).

            I pay withholding tax

            In most cantons church tax is included in the withholding tax. After leaving, you can ask the cantonal tax office to refund the church tax share. In the canton of St. Gallen the deadline is 31 March of the following year.

            In the cantons of Ticino and Geneva church tax is not included in the withholding tax.

            I have my own company (AG, GmbH)

            Leaving personally does not change the church tax of your company. According to the Federal Supreme Court, legal entities cannot rely on freedom of religion and therefore cannot leave. The only exception is organisations that themselves pursue religious purposes.

            Basel-Stadt, Schaffhausen, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, Aargau and Geneva do not levy church tax on companies. In Solothurn and St. Gallen part of the company taxes goes to the financial equalisation between parishes instead.

            My tax assessment still includes church tax

            Check the next assessment after leaving. If it is not yet final, you can file an objection within the deadline. Enclose the confirmation of leaving or the postal receipt.

            For assessments that are already final, a refund is only possible to a limited extent. It is expressly provided for by law in Nidwalden, Graubünden and Jura; in Zurich, Basel-Stadt and St. Gallen it goes through a revision or correction.

            Vaud, Neuchâtel, Geneva, Ticino, Valais: it works differently here

            Vaud: there is no church tax. The churches are funded from general taxes. If you do not belong to a recognised church, you can reclaim the church share of the municipal tax, once per tax period.

            Neuchâtel and Geneva: church tax is invoiced, but payment is voluntary.

            Ticino: only some parishes levy church tax. If you are entered in the register, you can request exemption in writing within 30 days. Later, a declaration takes effect for the following year.

            Valais: the municipalities bear the costs of worship. Only a few municipalities, including Sion, levy their own worship tax.

            Questions and answers

            Here are the key answers. One distinction helps: state law governs membership of the public-law corporation and tax liability, while church law (canon law, sacramental order) governs life in the community of faith.

            What is a partial withdrawal (Teilaustritt)?

            Many cantons have a dual system: alongside the Roman Catholic Church as a religious community there are public-law church corporations (cantonal churches and parishes, known as Landeskirchen and Kirchgemeinden). They are organised under public law and are funded, among other things, by church tax.

            With a partial withdrawal you leave this public-law corporation, without necessarily breaking with the community of faith.

            Can I still receive the sacraments or be a godparent?

            Yes. Leaving the parish and the cantonal church is separate from membership of the Roman Catholic Church. Canonical sanctions would only come into question if the person also made clear that they no longer accept central tenets of the faith.

            Where do I send the letter?

            To the parish at your place of residence. The territorial principle applies: anyone who pays church tax is in almost all cases automatically a member of the parish where they live. Step 1 shows you the responsible parish with its postal address.

            What form is required?

            In German-speaking Switzerland and the Jura, the declaration must be sent in writing to the parish at your place of residence. For proof, preferably by registered mail or A-Post Plus.

            In some cantons the signature must also be officially certified by the municipality or a notary. Step 3 tells you whether this applies to your place of residence.

            Do I need a confirmation?

            It's useful because it records from when your exit applies and tax liability ends. To end membership, though, it's enough that your declaration demonstrably reached the right office.

            What happens to my data in the register?

            Data protection and register questions are central: entry, correction, deletion, information. You can add requests in the “Additional note” field.

            How reliable is the parish search?

            The mapping from place to municipality comes from the official Swiss locality directory (swisstopo). We collected the parishes and their postal addresses from the directories of the cantonal churches and parishes. Every entry shows its source and date.

            Parish names and addresses are given in the local official language. Some details are only available in German. Since addresses change, a quick look at the parish website before sending is worth it. Please report errors to redaktion@verafides.ch.

            Does this also apply to Reformed or Christ Catholic members?

            The parish search only covers Roman Catholic parishes. The legal questions on this page concern the Roman Catholic Church. Other cantonal churches have their own rules.
